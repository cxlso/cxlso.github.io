# Acknowledgment

This dissertation is the result of many forms of support: intellectual,
technical, institutional, administrative, and personal. It emerged
through conversations, collaborations, workshops, laboratories, teaching
environments, and friendships that shaped both the work and the person
completing it.

I would first like to express my deepest gratitude to Dr. Shermeen
Yousif, with whom my Ph.D. journey began. She trusted me, welcomed me at
Florida Atlantic University, and gave me the opportunity to enter a
research environment, at the Environmental Design and Natural Materials
Lab, where computational design, robotic fabrication, ecological
thinking, and artificial intelligence could be explored together.
Although institutional circumstances led her to move to the University
of Texas at Arlington before my committee was formally constituted, her
mentorship did not end there. We remained in close contact, continued to
collaborate on research, and her intellectual influence remains
foundational to this dissertation. Much of this work grew from the
research culture, methodological ambition, and generosity that she made
possible.

I am equally grateful to my current advisor and committee chair, Dr.
Dustin White, for his constant support, careful feedback, and sharp
technical and design insight. His guidance helped me strengthen the
dissertation both conceptually and materially. He supported the
hands-on, experimental, and sometimes difficult nature of this work,
while continually helping me clarify its design logic, fabrication
relevance, and broader architectural contribution. His trust and
encouragement were essential during the final stages of this Ph.D.

I would also like to thank my dissertation committee for bringing
together a diverse constellation of perspectives across computation,
environmental humanities, fabrication, ethics, and architectural
research. Dr. Stacey Balkan brought an essential ethical and ecological
dimension to this work. Her perspective helped me understand that while
architecture can transform dreams into realities, it must first dream of
more sustainable futures. Provides Ng shaped my thinking about digital
commons, participation, decentralization, and collective authorship. Her
influence helped me understand architecture not only as an object-based
practice but as a medium through which shared agency can be organized.
Dr. Miktha Alkadri contributed a precise and grounded understanding of
performance, computation, and climate-responsive design. Our
collaboration on the robotic fabrication workshop in Indonesia
strengthened my commitment to contextually grounded, accessible, and
performative architecture. I am also grateful to Dr. Shermeen Yousif in
her role as honorary reviewer and continued mentor, whose work on
artificial intelligence, generative design, and performance-based
computation continues to inform the methodological and conceptual
trajectory of this dissertation.

I would like to thank Dr. Damla Soyer, my dear friend, for being the
first person with a Ph.D. in design whose path felt close enough for me
to imagine my own. Her support, conversations, and understanding helped
me navigate the emotional and intellectual difficulties of this journey.
I also thank Milad Gholamifard, Hassan Pishahang, and Maryam Badie for
their friendship, solidarity, and mutual support as part of the first
cohort of this program. Sharing the uncertainties, pressures, and
discoveries of this process with them made the journey less solitary.

I am grateful to Dr. Eric Berlatsky for supporting this new track and
for being present whenever I needed administrative help, institutional
guidance, or advice in moments of decision. His understanding and

I would like to acknowledge the FAU School of Architecture for providing

Special thanks go to Dr. Luigi Pacheco, Director of the Interactive

I would also like to thank Professor David Hall, who has been a true
inspiration and mentor over many years. His artistic vision,

I am deeply grateful to my friend Ahmed Semmoud, a true searcher for

Finally, I thank all the students, collaborators, workshop participants,
and colleagues who engaged with the experiments, tools, prototypes, and

spirit.
